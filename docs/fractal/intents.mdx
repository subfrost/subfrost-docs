# Intent Routes

A gateway address commits to an [intent](./overview#intents): a recipient,
and an optional **action**. With no action, the signer group mints plain
frFB to the recipient. With an action, the group **carries the action out on
Bitcoin as part of the mint**. The depositor (a UniSat user, an exchange
withdrawal, a script) sends FB to the gateway address and signs nothing
else.

Because the action is part of the intent bytes, it is part of the address:
each (route, recipient, bounds) has its own deposit address.

## The routes

An action is version byte `0x01`, a kind byte, then the kind's fields as
LEB128-encoded integers. There must be exactly that many fields, with
nothing after them.

| kind | route | fields | what the depositor ends up with |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1` | **BTC** | `min_frbtc` | frFB → frBTC on SUBFROSTAMM (`2:102671`), then unwrapped. BTC is paid to the recipient by the frBTC signer. |
| `2` | **Stable** | `min_frbtc, min_frusd, n, burn_args[n]` | frFB → frBTC → frUSD on the BTCUSD pool (`4:1778`), then frUSD burn-and-bridge (`4:1776`, opcode 14) to USDC or USDT on an EVM chain |
| `3` | **DIESEL** | `min_frbtc, min_diesel` | frFB → frBTC → DIESEL (OYL pool `2:77087`) |
| `4` | **LP** | `swap_ppm, min_frbtc, a_min_frfb, b_min_frbtc, min_lp` | part of the frFB swapped to frBTC, then both added to the frFB/frBTC pool. The LP token (`2:102671`) and any excess of either side go to the recipient. |

- **`burn_args`** (Stable) are the frUSD burn arguments after the target
  and opcode: the EVM recipient, the asset (0 USDC, 1 USDT), and the
  peg-out router payload (route, recipient, refund address, fee, deadline).
  The burn takes all the frUSD it receives and needs at least 10 frUSD.
- **`swap_ppm`** (LP) is the share of the frFB to swap, in parts per
  million. The single-sided zap split for a deposit of `A` into reserves
  `R` with fee fraction `f` (taking `g = 1 − f`) is:

  ```text
  s = (√(R²(1+g)² + 4gRA) − R(1+g)) / 2g
  ```
- **Contract ids are not in the action.** The signer group takes them from
  its own configuration. The action only names a route and its bounds, and
  the group wires every transfer itself, so an action can't move anything
  but its own deposit's frFB.
- **The recipient must be a Bitcoin script the depositor controls**, even
  for a Stable route that pays out on an EVM chain. It is where every
  refund goes (see below).

## Setting the bounds

About 20 minutes pass between registering a gateway address and the mint:
the sweep, the signer group's confirmation depth, and a Bitcoin block. The
**client** quotes the route and writes **absolute minimums** into the action
with a generous margin. The signer group does not re-quote.

Quote for the amount that will actually be minted:

```text
minted = deposit − K − ⌊(deposit − K) × 0.30%⌋ − 1,000 − route fee
```

Then, for each leg:

| leg | quote |
|---|---|
| SUBFROSTAMM swap | `out = in·(1e6 − fee)·R_out / (R_in·1e6 + in·(1e6 − fee))`. The pool's opcode 97 returns `reserve0, reserve1, lp_ppm, protocol_ppm, season_ppm`; fee = the sum of the three. For frFB/frBTC the reserves are (frFB, frBTC). |
| BTCUSD exchange | `get_dy(0, 1, dx)`, opcode 107 on `4:1778` |
| OYL swap | constant product at 1%; pool `2:77087` opcode 97 returns (DIESEL, frBTC) |
| LP | the zap split above, then the amounts added and the LP minted from the post-swap reserves and the pool's LP supply (opcode 101) |

Take **3–5%** off each. A bound that is too tight costs nothing but the
route (the deposit is then delivered as plain frFB); one that is too loose
costs only price.

## What happens, and what lands where

| case | the recipient receives |
|---|---|
| the route completes | BTC / the bridged stablecoin / DIESEL / LP tokens and any excess |
| the route **would** fail when the group checks it before signing | **plain frFB**. The group replays the exact transaction first; if any leg would revert, or the replay can't be read, it drops the route and mints normally |
| the route fails **on-chain** (the price moved past a bound after signing) | whatever the route had reached: frFB if the first leg failed, frBTC or frUSD if a later one did. Every leg refunds to the recipient. |
| the action doesn't decode | plain frFB |

**Nothing is lost to a route.** The worst case is the depositor holding frFB
(or the intermediate token) at their own address.

## On-chain shape

Alkanes gives every transaction at least 3.5M fuel however small it is, and
only a share of more depending on how busy the block is. So the signer group
does not bet on block share. It measures each leg's fuel in its pre-signing
replay and packs legs into transactions that each stay under the free
amount.

Measured on mainnet:

| leg | fuel |
|---|---|
| mint | 0.38M |
| SUBFROSTAMM swap | 2.15M |
| add liquidity | 2.69M |
| BTCUSD exchange | 4.67M |
| OYL swap | 4.67M |
| frUSD burn | 0.91M |
| unwrap | 0.51M |

- **Composed**, when everything fits: one mint transaction carrying the
  route.
- **Split**, when it doesn't (today, every route): the mint carries the
  first swap and a small group-held **escrow** output, pre-funded with the
  BTC the rest will spend. Child transactions spend the escrow and run the
  remaining legs, each paying for its parent (CPFP), so they normally
  confirm together.

  ```text
  mint     Mint · routing · first swap ─▶ escrow (group)
  child 1  escrow ─▶ next leg(s) ─▶ escrow′ (group) or the recipient
  child 2  …
  ```

  Every child has the recipient's output first, and every leg refunds
  there. If a child ever failed to land, the group would send the escrow,
  whole, to the recipient before minting anything else.
- **One routed deposit per mint.** A second one is minted next.

## Tracking a routed deposit

- **On Fractal:** exactly as for a plain deposit. See the
  [developer guide](./developer-guide).
- **The mint:** found the same way, by its `Mint` marker covering the
  deposit. A routed deposit's frFB is **not** sent to its output directly.
  It goes into the first route leg, or into the escrow when split. Identify
  the routed delivery as the output the route legs refund to.
- **Split routes:** the escrow is the group's output after the change
  output. Follow its spend to the first child, then each child's group
  output to the next. The last child's first output holds the result.
- **BTC route:** the BTC arrives afterwards, from the frBTC signer, at the
  recipient.
- **Stable route:** the payout then follows the frUSD peg-out, keyed by the
  burn transaction.
- **A dropped route** looks exactly like a plain deposit: frFB at the
  recipient, in the mint.

## Fees

| | amount (current settings) |
|---|---|
| route fee | 0 frFB per routed deposit. The signer group pays the Bitcoin fees of the extra legs. |
| swaps, exchange, bridge | each venue's own fee: 1.5% SUBFROSTAMM, the BTCUSD pool's, 1% OYL, the peg-out router's |

## Contract notes

- **SUBFROSTAMM swaps use opcode 29** (swap everything received). Opcode 13
  with `amount_in = 0` runs out of fuel on mainnet. Pass an explicit amount
  if you use opcode 13 yourself.
- **SUBFROSTAMM deadlines:** a deadline of 0 always reverts; routes pass the
  maximum value.
- **frBTC unwrap** must be a top-level protostone with no edicts, in a
  transaction with an output paying the frBTC signer. The group adds that
  output.
