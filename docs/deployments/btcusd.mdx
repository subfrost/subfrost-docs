# BTCUSD

Verified against Bitcoin mainnet on **2026-10-04** at block **969,906**.

## What BTCUSD is

**BTCUSD is a market, not a token.** There is no BTCUSD asset to hold. It is the
**frBTC/frUSD CryptoSwap pool** at [`4:1778`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:1778) -
Bitcoin priced in dollars, both sides of it being SUBFROST assets.

This matters for integrators because the natural assumption is wrong in a specific
way: you do not mint or redeem BTCUSD, you **trade the pool**, and the thing people
mean by "the BTCUSD price" is a view call on it.

| Contract | Id | Explorer |
|---|---|---|
| BTCUSD pool (frBTC/frUSD CryptoSwap) | `4:1778` | [explorer](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:1778) |

`4:1778` is a proxy and advertises only the four proxy opcodes. The pool views below
are served by the CryptoSwap implementation behind it, so do not expect to discover
them by reading the proxy's ABI.

## Reading BTC/USD

The call is **opcode 104**, `price_oracle`, an exponential moving average over
recent trades.

```bash
RPC=https://mainnet.subfrost.io/v4/subfrost/jsonrpc

alkanes-cli -p mainnet --jsonrpc-url "$RPC" alkanes simulate 4:1778:104
```

:::danger[BTC/USD is the reciprocal of what comes back]
`price_oracle` returns the price of coin1 (frUSD) denominated in coin0 (frBTC). To
get dollars per BTC you must invert it:

```
btc_usd = 1e18 / price_oracle
```

At block 969,906 the raw value was `11765042388081`, which is
**$84,997.57 per BTC** - not $117,650, which is what you get by dividing by
1e8 and is the single most common way to misread this feed.
:::

Two more opcodes exist and neither is the one you want for a spot price:

- **105, `price_scale`** - the repeg anchor. It lags a move *by design*.
- **106, `lp_price`** - the LP token value, not the pair price.

For pool composition use **opcode 999, `POOL_DETAILS`**. The standard
`GetTotalSupply` at opcode `101` **reverts** on these pools; it is not that the pool
has no supply, it is that the call is not implemented, and a client that treats a
revert as a zero will report an empty pool.

If you want BTC/USD with a freshness stamp rather than a bare number, read the
oracle's `btc` feed instead - it is a median of samples from this same pool, and it
tells you the height it was written at. See [oracles](./oracles).

## How BTCUSD is kept on peg

A pool price is only the price of the last trade in it. Left alone, a venue whose
depth is thinner than the wider market drifts away from it, and the drift is a
standing invitation to arbitrage at the expense of whoever is providing liquidity.

SUBFROST runs a **peg-keeping process** against this market. It continuously
compares the pool's price with external BTC/USD references, and when the two diverge
far enough to be worth acting on, it trades against the pool in the correcting
direction. Profitable divergence is closed by the protocol rather than by an
outsider, and the pool tracks the broader market without anyone being asked to
subsidise it.

What this does and does not promise:

- It **narrows** divergence. It does not pin the price, and there is no mechanism
  that forces the pool to a reference value.
- It acts **when correction is worth its own cost**. A divergence smaller than the
  fees and Bitcoin network cost of correcting it is left alone, so a small, persistent
  spread is the expected resting state and not a fault.
- It is **best-effort**. It depends on a process that can be stopped, on blockspace
  being affordable, and on its own capital. None of those is guaranteed on any
  particular block.

So: do not treat the pool price as an oracle-grade reference for a contract that
settles money. For that, read [the oracle](./oracles), which gives you a height and
lets you set your own staleness tolerance.

## Trading it

Trade `4:1778` as an ordinary CryptoSwap pool: frBTC in for frUSD out, or the
reverse. You need [frBTC](./frbtc) or [frUSD](./frusd) first - there is no path that
starts from bare BTC in a single call.
