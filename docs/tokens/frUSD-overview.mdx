# frUSD Overview
## The Dollar, Settled on Bitcoin L1

`frUSD` is a US-dollar token that lives on Bitcoin L1 as an [Alkanes](../protocol/alkanes) asset. It holds an ordinary Bitcoin UTXO, moves in an ordinary Bitcoin transaction, and can be composed into the same contract calls as any other Alkanes token. That is what separates it from a dollar that merely *references* Bitcoin from another chain.

It is live on mainnet at alkane id `4:1776`, with **8 decimals**.

The point of frUSD is a BTC/USD market that clears on proof-of-work. The dollars most of the world actually uses settle somewhere else and ask Bitcoin to wait; frUSD settles where the bitcoin already is.

## Where the dollars are

frUSD is **fully reserved, not algorithmic**. There is no on-chain collateral ratio to watch, no liquidation engine, and no seigniorage. Each frUSD exists because someone deposited a dollar-denominated stablecoin on Ethereum:

1. A user deposits **USDC or USDT** into the SUBFROST bridge vault on Ethereum.
2. The SUBFROST signer group observes the deposit and mints the matching `frUSD` on Bitcoin, to the Bitcoin address named in the deposit.
3. Burning `frUSD` on Bitcoin is the reverse instruction: it releases value back out on Ethereum.

Anchoring to USDC and USDT is a deliberate choice rather than a shortcut. On-chain, the canonical dollar is already settled: USDC and USDT are what the market quotes against and treats as cash. frUSD does not try to unseat that, it anchors to it, so the peg tracks the dollar the ecosystem has already agreed on instead of a synthetic approximation. What SUBFROST changes is not the dollar itself but the layer you hold and move it on.

The deposited stablecoins are not left idle. The vault's strategy contract deposits them into [Curve's 3pool](https://curve.fi/), so the reserve backing frUSD is held as 3pool LP rather than as raw USDC and USDT. Changing that strategy takes the same threshold signature as moving the collateral itself.

That shape is doing more work than earning a yield. A Curve LP token has no issuer, and so no function to freeze a balance: whatever authority a stablecoin issuer holds, they do not hold it over a pool share.

<Admonition type="warning" title="The trust model, stated plainly">

frUSD does not carry the same risk profile as `frBTC`, and the two should not be read as equally trust-minimized. The difference is not who holds the keys. It is where the collateral lives.

The reserve sits on Ethereum, in the SUBFROST bridge vault. That vault has no owner key: moving collateral out, changing the strategy and taking fees are all reachable only through a call carrying a BIP-340 signature from the same [FROST](../key-components/frost-and-roast) threshold group that signs frUSD's Bitcoin side. That group is frUSD's own; `frBTC` is signed by a separate federation.

What that does not remove:

- **The collateral is off Bitcoin.** It carries Ethereum risk, Curve risk, and stablecoin issuer risk that `frBTC` does not.
- **The contracts are upgradeable, and unaudited.** Both the vault and the `4:1776` token sit behind upgradeable proxies, and none of this has been through a formal security audit. The token also keeps an owner of its own, an auth token that sets parameters like the burn floor; that is the vault's asymmetry, not its shape.
- **A threshold is cryptography, not yet a federation.** How much a threshold protects you depends on where the key shares actually sit, and that is an operational fact rather than something you can read off a chain.
- **Minting and redeeming run on SUBFROST infrastructure.** The bridge screens deposits against sanctions lists at its edge and checks payout addresses against the stablecoin issuers' own blacklists. An exit also waits on the signer group to act, with no bound on how long, and the only way to submit its calls is a relayer set managed by a single admin address. Holding and moving frUSD on Bitcoin itself is screened by none of that, and has no freeze function.

Use it with all of that in mind.

</Admonition>

## How can I get frUSD?

### 1. Swap for it in the frUSD/frBTC pool

The most direct route, and the one that never leaves Bitcoin. `frUSD` trades against `frBTC` in a [CryptoSwap pool](../using-subfrost/pools-liquidity) at `4:1778`, so BTC-denominated value can be moved into dollars and back without touching another chain.

### 2. Mint it by bridging USDC or USDT from Ethereum

You deposit on Ethereum and the `frUSD` is minted to a Bitcoin address you name. The vault takes a flat fee and then a percentage of the remainder, currently **$1.99 plus 0.10%**, both read from the chain rather than hardcoded. **Burning back out pays the same flat fee and the same percentage**, so a burn near the floor returns noticeably less than it destroys.

That floor is a **minimum of 10 frUSD**: below it the burn reverts rather than paying short. It is the token's default rather than a constant, changeable by whoever holds its auth token and readable from the chain.

In the app, choose USDC or USDT as what you are paying with and `frUSD` as what you are receiving. You will be asked to connect an Ethereum wallet and sign two transactions: an approval for exactly the amount you are depositing, and the deposit itself.

If you would rather drive it yourself, [BTC/USD Commands](../api-reference/cli-sdk/btcusd) prints the same two calls for you to sign in your own wallet.

:::danger[A plain transfer to the vault is not a deposit]
The deposit must be made through the bridge contract's own deposit call, which carries the instruction naming your Bitcoin address. **Sending USDC or USDT to the vault address as an ordinary token transfer does not mint anything**: it arrives with no instruction attached, and there is no automatic route back.
:::

### 3. Receive it

`frUSD` is an ordinary Alkanes token. Any wallet that handles Alkanes assets can hold, send, and receive it, and it shows up alongside your other balances.

## What is live today

Being specific about status matters, so here is where each piece stands.

| | Status |
|---|---|
| The `frUSD` token on Bitcoin mainnet (`4:1776`) | **Live** |
| Minting by bridging USDC/USDT from Ethereum | **Live**. The command line prepares the two Ethereum calls for you to sign in your own wallet |
| Burning frUSD back out to Ethereum | **Live** |
| Splitting part of a deposit into BTC on the way in | **Live**. A split the network cannot execute pays plain frUSD instead, so read the notes on [BTC/USD Commands](../api-reference/cli-sdk/btcusd) before using it |
