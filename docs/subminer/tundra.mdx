# TUNDRA and the recoup loop

TUNDRA is a permissionless over-collateralised lending market on Bitcoin L1,
built on alkanes. It is where SUBMINER's treasury borrows the BTC that funds its
relayer fleet — and therefore where anyone who wants to **earn yield on BTC** can
take the other side of that trade.

Lending in the SUBFROST app settles through TUNDRA. It replaced an earlier
peer-to-peer loan market, where a lender and a borrower agreed fixed terms and
matched maker-to-taker, which the app no longer offers. TUNDRA is pool-based
instead: one pool contract per market, shared by every supplier and borrower in
it, with rates that float on utilisation, interest that accrues continuously,
and positions that can be liquidated. Other pages in these docs still describe
the earlier design; where they disagree with this page, this page is current.

:::info[Verified against mainnet on 2026-10-04]
Contract ids and parameters below were read from the deployment record and

[`4:881050`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:881050) on the explorer before
relying on any of it.
:::

:::danger[No outside firm has audited TUNDRA]
This is the single most important sentence on this page. The deployment's own

still outstanding** — a deliberate decision, recorded as such, not something a
script relaxed on its own. One adversarial review pass has been run internally

audit. Size your exposure accordingly.

## Architecture

TUNDRA is shaped like Morpho Blue rather than like Compound: there is no
singleton holding every market behind a shared ledger. Instead a **factory**
clones **one pool per market**, and each pool physically holds only its own
market's collateral.

That choice is the headline property. Under a singleton, a bug in one market's
callee has a blast radius of every market at once. Here it is one pool, full
stop.

| Property | TUNDRA |
|---|---|
| Market creation | **permissionless**, market id = hash(params) |
| Risk parameters | **frozen at creation**, unchangeable by anyone |
| Pause | **does not exist** |
| Liquidation | closed-form discount from LLTV, no auction, no close factor |
| Bad debt | **socialised onto suppliers** in the same call, immediately and visibly |
| Governance | one bearer token, limited to enable-lists and fee knobs |

Read that table as a risk disclosure, not a feature list. "No pause" means
nobody can rescue you. "Bad debt socialised immediately" means a liquidation
that cannot complete is absorbed by suppliers in that market, visibly, in the
transaction that discovers it. These are the costs of a market nobody can
intervene in; they are the same reason nobody can intervene against you.

## Earning yield: supplying to a market

This is the participation path. You supply the market's **loan token** and earn
interest paid by borrowers.

### Getting frBTC

The DIESEL/frBTC market lends **frBTC**, SUBFROST's wrapped Bitcoin, at
[`32:0`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/32:0). You hold native BTC, so the
first step is wrapping it:

- [frBTC overview](../tokens/frBTC-overview) — what it is and how custody works
- [Wrapping frBTC](../developer-guide/wrapping-frBTC) — the mechanics

frBTC is redeemable for native BTC, so the round trip BTC → frBTC → supply →
withdraw → BTC leaves you in the asset you started in, plus interest.

### Supply and withdraw

| Opcode | Name | What rides in | What rides out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10** | `Supply` | the loan token (frBTC) | pool share units |
| **11** | `Withdraw` | pool share units (burned) | the loan token |

Both take **no amount argument**. Whatever you attach to the call is what is
supplied; the attached parcel *is* the amount, so an explicit argument would
only restate it. Likewise on withdraw, the share units you send in are the
authorisation — there is nothing else to check, because holding the shares is
the claim.

The pool's **own alkane units are the share token**. It mints and burns them
in-process; there is no separate share contract and no external mint opcode. So
after supplying to [`2:98619`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/2:98619) you
hold units of `2:98619` itself, and your balance of them is your claim on the
market's supply side.

Shares appreciate against the loan token as interest accrues, rather than
rebasing. Your unit count stays fixed and each unit becomes redeemable for more
frBTC.

### Where the yield comes from

Interest is set by the shared **adaptive-curve IRM** at
[`4:674184`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:674184), which targets a
utilisation level and moves the rate toward it over time — the rate rises while
the market is borrowed above target and falls below it. Rate state is keyed by
`context.caller`, so the calling pool *is* the market and each market carries its
own curve.

The borrower paying that interest in the DIESEL/frBTC market is, today, the
SUBMINER treasury — borrowing against DIESEL collected from users who paid their
Bitcoin fees in DIESEL. **That is the sense in which this yield is real**: it
traces to demand for Bitcoin blockspace rather than to an emission schedule.

The protocol takes a fee of **10%** of interest from creation, claimable by the
fee recipient via `ClaimFee` (opcode 19).

### Verify a pool before you supply

Market creation is permissionless, so anyone can deploy a pool that looks
exactly like a real one. The trust root is the factory's registry, and the check
is a **two-way round trip** — both halves, not either one:

```text
pool.GetFactory()                    == 4:881050      # opcode 998
factory.GetPool(pool.GetMarketId())  == pool          # opcode 999 then the factory
```

A contract can claim any factory it likes; what it cannot do is make the real
factory's registry name it back. Run both directions before sending funds.

### Views

| Op | Name | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| 99 / 100 / 101 | `GetName` / `GetSymbol` / `GetTotalSupply` | the ecosystem token ABI |
| 102 | `GetMarketParams` | the nine-word creation tuple |
| 103 | `GetMarket` | market state, six LE u128 words |
| 104 | `GetPosition` | `borrow_shares`, `collateral`, tagged owner |
| 105 | `GetPositionKeyAt` | the nth 32-byte position key |
| 109 | `GetPositionCount` | positions ever opened |

| 998 / 999 | `GetFactory` / `GetMarketId` | the discovery round trip |

:::note[Why `GetMarket` and `GetPosition` are 103 and 104]
99, 100 and 101 are the ecosystem token ABI that every wallet and indexer
hardcodes for any alkane. Because a pool's own units are its share token, it
must answer those — so the market views that would naturally sit at 100 and 101
are displaced to 103 and 104.
:::

### Borrowing, for implementers

`OpenBorrow` (12) takes collateral in and sends the loan out in **one call**.
There is deliberately no way to add debt to a position that already exists: if
there were, anyone could borrow against someone else's collateral up to the
health limit. The proceeds would still route to the owner, so nothing is stolen
— but the owner would be left sitting on the liquidation boundary for the
attacker to liquidate.

`owner_kind` selects how the position's owner is recorded:

- **kind 0** — the owner is the scriptPubKey of output `owner_output`. The
  pointer must pay that same output, which makes the designation
  self-validating: a scriptPubKey that cannot receive the loan cannot be
  recorded, so a typo cannot strand a position forever.
- **kind 1** — `context.caller` is the owner and `owner_output` is ignored.

`Repay` (13), `AddCollateral` (14) and `ClosePosition` (15) are all
**permissionless to call**. Repaying someone's debt or topping up their
collateral can only help them, and a close releases collateral only to the
recorded owner — so anyone may trigger these and nobody but the owner benefits.

:::warning[Zero-valued arguments are invisible to a hand-decode]
`OpenBorrow`'s `owner_kind`, `owner_output` and `salt` are frequently all zero,
which is byte-identical to the zero padding alkanes applies to calldata on the
wire. A hand-decode cannot distinguish a correct five-word cellpack from one
missing three trailing parameters — the re-encode check passes either way. Only
a **simulation against the real WASM** discriminates. Never treat a clean
hand-decode of a zero-padded cellpack as proof it is complete.
:::

## The DIESEL/frBTC market

The market SUBMINER borrows from.

| Parameter | Value |

| Collateral | DIESEL [`2:0`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/2:0) |
| Loan | frBTC [`32:0`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/32:0) |
| LLTV | **0.40** |
| Minimum borrow | **3,500,000 sats** |
| Created | 2026-09-27, block 968,870 |
| Market id | `22fd73d6dd54b57378d4ce1c565406053933739f6fd4a2d3643556e3bd470d5a` |

The sibling DIESEL/frUSD market at
[`2:98445`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/2:98445) has LLTV 0.40, a minimum
borrow of 3,000 frUSD, and a 10% fee from creation. Its market id is
`969751733f39479fa2c4291126ec4df55b8670e9e3a901c14a2d095532a5f573`.

Maximum borrow is a double-floored computation:

```text
max_borrow = floor( floor(collateral * price / 1e27) * lltv / 1e18 )
```

The **3,500,000-sat minimum borrow is the binding constraint** on the SUBMINER
side: at LLTV 0.40 it takes roughly 386 DIESEL of collateral before a borrow
clears the floor at all. The market leaves no smaller first position available,
which is why the treasury's borrow policy is sized to the whole balance rather
than to a cautious opening bet.

### The oracle, and why it can freeze things

Prices come from a **TUNDRACLE adapter**, which reads the TUNDRACLE proxy at
[`4:9010`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:9010).

The adapter returns a **ratio of two feed keys, not a USD price.** Freshness
therefore depends on both legs. A price that cannot be read **freezes
liquidation exactly as surely as it freezes borrowing** — the market does not
degrade gracefully into "liquidations still work," it stops. The correct
response is to keep a position far from the LLTV boundary rather than merely to
check staleness before drawing.

### Liquidation

`Liquidate` (16) seizes collateral from an unhealthy position at a fixed
discount. Exactly one of `seized_assets` and `repaid_shares` may be non-zero.

With `callee` set, the seized collateral is handed to the callee first and the
repayment comes out of what the callee returns — which is how a liquidator
holding no loan-token inventory funds the repayment. With `callee` zero, the
loan token must ride in. The deployed callee is
[`4:962137`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:962137), which routes through
an AMM venue at [`4:65522`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/4:65522).

## The recoup loop

How SUBMINER's treasury turns collected DIESEL into relayer funding, without
ever selling the DIESEL.

| Leg | What it does | State on 2026-10-04 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | sweep DIESEL out of the treasury | ✅ **settled on mainnet** |
| 1b | second sweep, 60 DIESEL | ✅ **settled on mainnet** |
| 2 | post the DIESEL as TUNDRA collateral | 🔴 code written, never executed |
| 3 | `OpenBorrow` frBTC against it | 🔴 code written, never executed |
| 4 | unwrap the frBTC to native BTC | 🔴 code written, never executed |
| 5 | deliver the BTC to the FROST group funding relayers | 🔴 code written, never executed |

Treasury state, read from
[`2:96782`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/2:96782):

- **held** (opcode 100) = 46,046,327,487 base units = **460.46 DIESEL**
- **deposited** (opcode 102) = the same number — nothing collected yet
- **payee** (opcode 101) = [`2:96775`](https://explorer.subfrost.io/alkane/2:96775)

:::tip[Why those two numbers being equal is the real check]
Opcode 100 is a live read of the contract's actual balance. Opcode 102 is a
lifetime counter that only the `deposit()` opcode advances. A deposit routed
properly moves **both**; an alkane merely edicted at the treasury moves only the
balance and leaves the counter flat. A divergence between them is therefore the
signature of a stranded edict — which is precisely the condition this treasury
fork exists to detect and recover. Compare them on every reading.
:::

So the loop is **half-proven**: DIESEL demonstrably accumulates in the treasury
from real mainnet transactions, and the borrow side is written but has not yet
drawn its first satoshi. Until leg 3 executes, the relayer fleet is funded
directly rather than from borrowed frBTC.

## Risk summary

Stated plainly, because supplying capital on the strength of a documentation
page is how people lose money.

1. **No external audit.** One internal adversarial pass, which found and fixed a
   critical. `HOLD_UNTIL_AUDIT` was cleared by owner decision with the audit
   outstanding.
2. **No pause, no admin rescue.** Risk parameters are frozen at market creation
   and cannot be retuned by anyone, including the protocol.
3. **Bad debt is socialised onto suppliers** in the same call that discovers it.
4. **Oracle dependency.** The price is a ratio of two feeds; if it cannot be
   read, liquidations stop as well as borrows.
5. **Permissionless market creation.** Always run the factory round trip before
   trusting a pool id.
6. **Counterparty concentration.** In the DIESEL/frBTC market the borrower is
   substantially one party — the SUBMINER treasury — collateralised by one
   asset, DIESEL, whose price is itself correlated with Bitcoin fee-market
   activity.

Point 6 deserves the extra beat: the thing that makes this yield real — that it
is paid out of Bitcoin blockspace demand — is the same thing that correlates the
collateral with the borrower's ability to pay.
